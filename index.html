About

I hold an MSc in Industrial Analytics (Industrial Engineering and Management) from Uppsala University, Sweden, and a BSc in Industrial and Production Engineering from the University of Dhaka, Bangladesh.

My research asks how far a large language model can be allowed into a manufacturing simulation pipeline while the result stays verifiable. Language models can write simulation code and suggest improvements, but they do so probabilistically, so their output can look correct and still be wrong. My approach is to place a deterministic checking layer, built from knowledge graphs and formal constraints, between the model and the decision.

Before my master's degree I worked for two years as an industrial engineer at Ovi Fashion Ltd in Bangladesh, carrying out time-and-motion studies across nine production lines.

MSc thesis

Gen_AI_DES: Automated Generation and Constrained Optimization of Manufacturing Simulations Using Large Language Models and Knowledge Graphs

Uppsala University, May 2026. 30 credits. With Qile Luo. Supervisor: Dr Kaveh Amouzgar.

The thesis addresses the reliability of manufacturing simulation models generated by large language models, and presents two complementary approaches. My part was the first. A production event log is processed with process mining to extract the structure of the line: machines, sequence, cycle times, availability and shift times. This is encoded as a knowledge graph (RDF and SPARQL) and translated into SHACL constraints describing what a correct model must contain. Each simulation model the language model writes is checked against these constraints before it runs, and any violation is returned to the model for correction. The same constraints screen the improvements the model proposes, within operator-defined limits, before they are simulated and ranked. The pipeline is delivered as a no-code application. This work became the basis of the EUROSIM 2026 paper below.

Current work

With Dr Kaveh Amouzgar and Thomas Schmitt, I am extending this work from verifying the model a language model builds to verifying the recommendation a simulation pipeline makes. A recommendation must pass a deterministic decision-validity gate (covering efficiency, materiality beyond noise, robustness to weighting and stability across data) before a language model may explain it. A manuscript is in preparation.

Publication

Amouzgar, K., Roy, A., and Schmitt, T. (2026). Knowledge-Graph-Based Verification for Reliable LLM-Driven Discrete-Event Simulation in Manufacturing. Procedia Computer Science (EUROSIM 2026).

Peer-reviewed. Presented at the 12th EUROSIM Congress, Genoa, Italy, 21–23 September 2026. Open access.

The paper adds a deterministic verification layer to LLM-driven discrete-event simulation. A formal specification derived from event-log data is encoded as a knowledge graph and translated into SHACL constraints, and every generated model is checked against them before execution. On a seven-machine automotive production line, the framework corrected a structural error in the first generated model without human intervention, and agreed with an independent commercial simulation tool to within 4.7%. A sensitivity analysis showed that operator-defined constraints were the main factor shaping achievable improvements, which reached up to 9.2% higher throughput and 6.0% lower energy use.
